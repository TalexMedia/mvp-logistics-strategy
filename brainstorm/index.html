1 Where MVP's Video Is Today · 5 min

What's Already Out There

What we found
  • None of the 21 website pages we checked has a video on it, and the four case studies are written.

2 Who MVP Wants To Reach · 10 min

The People Watching

What we heard
  • MVP's buyers are CEOs, presidents and VPs of supply chain at mid-market food, beverage and manufacturing companies.
  • Carsten told us MVP doesn't want to compete on transportation rates. The aim is to take cost out of the supply chain, or help the customer use the supply chain as an advantage.
To talk about
  1. What does a CEO or president want from a series, and what does a VP of supply chain want?
  2. What do your buyers need to see from MVP before they get in touch?
  3. Where are your buyers most likely to come across MVP's videos?

4 Idea One · 10 min

A Returning Documentary Series

Format
A documentary series focused on telling one story, from start to finish, per episode.
A company that did it
Sequoia Capital, Crucible Moments. A professional services firm made a series about the turning points its clients lived through and hosted by one of its partners.
Episode length
10-20 minutes per episode

Episode length is our suggestion for MVP.

Watch an episode on YouTube
A starting point for MVP

Each episode could follow one company's supply chain decision through to what happened afterwards. One episode could follow a food company that opened a new distribution hub, and another, a manufacturer that replaced several logistics providers with one.

To talk about
  1. Which customer decisions would your buyers most want to see from the inside?
  2. Which customers might be open to telling their story on camera?
  3. How could the Ask Me Anything series and a documentary series work alongside each other?

5 Idea Two · 10 min

A Scripted Workplace Comedy

Format
A short form scripted series designed to be easy to consume on the go (aka a Taco Drama) following a standard sitcom format.
A company that did it
FloQast, PBC. An accounting software company made a scripted comedy about the working life of auditors, for the accountants and finance teams it sells to, and put it out under its own studio name.
Episode length
1-2 minutes per episode

Episode length is our suggestion for MVP.

Watch an episode on YouTube
A starting point for MVP

The series could follow a fictionalized supply chain team at a mid-market food manufacturer. Another option is a fictionalized logistics team helping a growing brand open in new markets. MVP's October 20 LinkedIn post already uses humor, with the line "Fuel prices doing their thing."

To talk about
  1. Which moments in a supply chain team's week would your buyers recognize right away?
  2. How much humor fits the way MVP wants to sound?
